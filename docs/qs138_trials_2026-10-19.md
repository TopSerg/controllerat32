# QS138 / Gen1.1 stand trials, 2026-10-19

The left QS138 was driven by Gen1.1, and the coupled right motor by the
Sintech inverter and its pedal. The Gen1.1 DC link was approximately 60 V.
The stand operator confirmed that the shafts were coupled and emergency stop
was available. Both inverters are powered from a laboratory supply; the
available regenerative energy absorption and its rating are not established.
The present commissioning profile limits phase current to 10 A, DC charge
current to 1 A, and raw
overcurrent to 20 A (`user/qs138Commissioning.h`).

## Firmware and command stop

The first 5 A no-load test spun the left motor evenly, but it continued to
drive after the client sent `Stop`. The operator removed and reapplied DC
power. Inspection found that `timerTickCall()` in `user/main.c` did not clear
`TestRefSignals` when the CAN current command was disabled. The generated
`Identification` CURRENT path retains those signals. The firmware now clears
the active flag, Id, Iq, and voltage/angle overrides on disable.

The updated `build/debug/AT32_Base.bin` was built with
`cmake --build --preset debug`, flashed through PCAN, and confirmed running on
CAN. SHA-256:
`B32CBCC99CCA2CC9B4646580A9276550D71AED49E576FDDE1C5CE70778F4E814`.
The flash transcript is `test_logs/flash_stopfix_20261019.log`. The previous
binary was saved as `test_logs/AT32_Base_before_stopfix_20261019.bin`.

The capture client now sends `Iq=0` at the end of a trial, continues CAN
command transmission and telemetry, and only disconnects after measured
current is at most 2 A and speed at most 25 rpm for two seconds. This stop
was confirmed by telemetry and the operator after the new 5 A no-load run.

## Trials with updated firmware

| Trial | Operator observation | Telemetry / diagnostic result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No load, 2 A for 3 s | Audible attempt to move; no rotation | 0 rpm peak; zero command and stop confirmed; no fault |
| No load, 5 A for 4 s | Smooth rotation, then stopped with no residual pull | 229 rpm peak; 6.1 A peak from 50 ms telemetry; zero `IqRefReg` at end; no fault |
| Pedal drive, 1 A for 3 s | No noticeable load; shafts stopped | -802 rpm peak; median `Iq=0.93 A` during 1 A request; signed `IdcFilt=-0.22 A` median; no fault |
| Pedal drive, 3 A for 3 s | Braking was felt as acceleration when load was removed with pedal unchanged | -1022 rpm peak **after** release; median `Iq=3.32 A` during 3 A request; signed `IdcFilt=-0.60 A` median; speed abort threshold crossed after current command returned to zero; no MCU fault |
| Pedal drive, 5 A; pedal released first | Braking felt, both shafts stopped | -697 rpm peak; median `Iq=5.07 A` while 5 A requested; signed `IdcFilt=-0.43 A` median; zero commanded at -306 rpm, stop verified; no abort or fault |

The 3 A speed excursion shows that `n_max` on Gen1.1 cannot cap speed imposed
by the right motor. Releasing the Gen1.1 braking current while keeping the
right pedal fixed allowed the coupled shaft to accelerate. For the 5 A run,
the operator released the right pedal first; the capture client kept the
braking request until speed fell below 350 rpm, then sent zero. This sequence
avoided the rebound.

In the 5 A run, `UdcFiltered` stayed between 60.15 and 60.70 V, and no DC
voltage limit activated. The DC current limiter was active in 157 of 427
20 ms snapshots with a 5 A reference (37%). The 1 A charge-current setting
therefore constrains sustained braking. The diagnostic `motorTorque` is a
model estimate, about 0.79 N m median at 5 A; there is no calibrated torque
transducer measurement. The right inverter's torque/current telemetry was
not recorded. The signed `IdcFilt` field indicates regeneration during the
load pulse, but its accuracy has not been independently calibrated.

The current regulator followed 1, 3, and 5 A references without a growing
error in these short, low-current runs. This does not establish stability at
larger currents or over a wider speed range. The installed current and DC
charge limits should remain until the Gen1.1 power-stage ratings and actual
energy sink ratings are known. The operator plans to run further independent
tests before the next joint session.

## Later attempted staircase: resolver feedback did not follow the shaft

In a later attempt, the operator drove the right motor and the torque-meter
coupling indicated about 550 rpm. The operator also confirmed that the left
motor shaft itself was visibly turning. Gen1.1 was armed with `Id=Iq=0`, but
its CAN speed remained exactly zero for all 92 s of capture. The raw resolver
SIN/COS values stayed near `280/-1711` ADC counts and its reported angle
varied by only 0.0037 rad; `ResolverSpeedValid` stayed false. Earlier successful
load captures show the angle traversing nearly the full `0..2*pi` range and
nonzero negative speed. The host sent no nonzero current command in this
attempt, then sent zero and Stop; the CAN trace has 0 nonzero 0x300 commands,
no write errors or fault frames, and peak sampled dq current of 2.24 A.

This rules out absence of a JTAG connection as the reason the speed was not
visible: the PCAN link delivered 50,445 frames including resolver telemetry.
The cause of the now static resolver signal remains to be determined at the
sensor, its wiring, excitation, ADC input, or sampling path. Do not resume
load-current trials until a passive rotation check shows changing raw SIN/COS
and valid shaft speed.

The attempted staircase is preserved under `test_logs/staircase_135_20261019_`
as raw `can.csv`, `telemetry.jsonl`, exported `telemetry.csv`, and decoded
`diagnostics.csv`.

After the operator manually reloaded and started the firmware, a second
60 s check repeated the discrepancy. Gen1.1 remained in FreeWheel (`gate=1`),
with current control disabled. The operator reported 400–600 rpm on the torque
meter and visually confirmed the left shaft rotating. The 1,122 telemetry
samples still showed exactly 0 rpm, `ResolverSpeedValid=false`, SIN from 1783
to 1791 and COS from -133 to -124 ADC counts, and only 0.0006 rad of reported
angle movement. Raw CAN recorded 4,573 disabled, zero-current 0x300 commands,
zero nonzero commands, zero write errors, and no reported faults. The new logs are
`test_logs/resolver_recheck_20261019_{can.csv,telemetry.jsonl,telemetry.csv}`.
The 1/3/5/7/10 A staircase and left-first trial were not attempted because
rotor-angle feedback was not trustworthy.

## Manual left-drive run after the passive checks

The operator subsequently drove the left motor from the Gen1.1 GUI and
exported `logbook_20261019_172120.csv`; a copy is in
`test_logs/manual_left_drive_20261019_172120.csv`. This 0.5 s GUI log shows
the left motor reaching 621 rpm under a positive `Iq` request. Across the
15 s segment requesting 10 A, measured `Iq` had median 6.2 A and maximum
7.6 A; speed derating was active in 29 of 31 samples, largely above 600 rpm.
The raw resolver SIN/COS columns traversed roughly -1778..1843 and
-1776..1907 counts, and the reported electrical speed reached 325 rad/s.
Other manually selected requests followed; the final 201 samples show
`Iq` request 0 and shaft speed 0, and the operator confirmed the shaft stopped.
One sample in the run reported CAN counter fault bit 2; the software,
hardware, and sensor fault fields were zero throughout.

This proves that angle feedback can track the rotor during self-drive, so a
permanent resolver disconnection is not supported. It does not explain why
both externally driven checks above showed essentially static SIN/COS while
the operator observed the left shaft turning. The next comparison should use
the same GUI session with `Id=Iq=0` while the right motor sweeps through a
range of speeds, then export that log. The mechanical coupling and any
measurement-path dependency on inverter state remain open questions.

That GUI comparison was exported as `logbook_20261019_172609.csv`, copied to
`test_logs/manual_external_zero_20261019_172609.csv`. Over 26 s, the right
motor drove the coupled shaft according to the operator and torque meter.
Gen1.1's GUI log showed -745..0 rpm, raw SIN/COS traversed broad ranges, and
the resolver angle traversed almost the full circle. All 53 samples retained
`Isq=0`, `gate=2`, 60 V DC bus, and no MCU fault. The operator released the
pedal and confirmed both shafts stopped. This shows that external rotation
can also be observed correctly with the GUI; the earlier static-signal
captures are intermittent or depend on a state not yet isolated. Do not
assume the underlying cause is fixed without another controlled capture.

## Manual GUI logs received later on 2026-10-19

The operator supplied three 0.5 s GUI exports in
`C:/Project/StandRumoteUI/client/`: `logbook_20261019_152702.csv`,
`logbook_20261019_152838.csv`, and `logbook_20261019_152946.csv`.
They begin after the right motor has already brought the coupled shaft to a
negative speed, so they do not show the full earlier startup sequence. Their
`Isq` column echoes the host request; `Iq` is measured current. The `TX.*`
columns are empty, and raw CAN/20 ms control snapshots were not captured.

| GUI export | Request and shaft speed | Measured response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `152702` | `Isq=-10 A`, -831 to -879 rpm | Median `Iq=+0.1 A`; speed derating in all 25 samples |
| `152838` | `Isq=-10 A`, near -611 rpm | Median `Iq=-6.3 A` over 28 samples at 610–620 rpm; speed derating active |
| `152838` | `Isq=-10 A`, faster than 640 rpm | Median `Iq=+0.2 A`; speed derating active |
| `152946` | `Isq=-10 A`, -840 to -974 rpm | Median `Iq=+0.3 A`; speed derating in all 15 samples |
| `152838`, `152946` | `Isq=+10 A` applied at negative speed | Measured `Iq` rose to about +9–10 A; shaft passed through zero and reached about +100 rpm |

The current regulator is not shown diverging in these exports. At negative
speed, negative `Iq` is a motoring request in the direction imposed by the
right motor; positive `Iq` is braking. The generated model compares
`abs(Control.Welectrical)` with `SystemParameters.FuncLimits.Speed_rev` for
motoring. With five pole pairs, the electrical speed is five times the
mechanical speed. The commissioning initialization puts the mechanical
3000 rpm value `314 rad/s` directly into `Speed_rev`, with a derating range of
`20 rad/s` (`user/main.c`, `user/qs138Commissioning.h`). Consequently the
actual motoring limit begins near **600 mechanical rpm** and reaches zero
around **638 rpm**. The 20 ms snapshots in our 5 A run confirm an exact
five-to-one `Welectrical/Wmechanical` ratio. The generated limit formula
predicts a current cap near 7 A at 611 rpm and zero above 638 rpm, matching
the manual exports. This electrical/mechanical unit mismatch should be
corrected in a separately reviewed firmware revision; no speed-limit change
was flashed during these trials.

The GUI `HostOverspeed` flag is separate: it appeared around 1000 rpm in the
manual logs. The MCU reported no CAN, software, hardware or sensor fault in
the three exports. The 0.5 s sampling does not show fast current transients,
and the empty `TX.*` columns prevent a direct check of transmitted 0x300
frames. A future comparison should capture raw CAN and `IqRefReg` while
applying a short, positive, low-current braking request at a controlled
negative shaft speed.
